How to Register a Trademark in China: A Step-by-Step Guide (2026)

China runs on a strict first-to-file principle, and foreign applicants without a local business address must file through a CNIPA-recorded Chinese agent. This guide walks through the seven practical steps — from searching and choosing classes to filing, examination and what happens if you receive a refusal — with current fees and a realistic timeline.

Before you start: three rules that shape everything

  1. First to file wins. In China, trademark rights generally belong to whoever files first, not whoever used the brand first. This is why trademark squatting is a real risk — waiting can mean losing your own name.
  2. Foreigners need a local agent. Applicants without a habitual residence or business address in China must be represented by an agency recorded with the China National Intellectual Property Administration (CNIPA).
  3. Rights are territorial. A US, EU or UK registration does not protect you in mainland China. Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan each have separate systems. You need either a Chinese national filing or a Madrid Protocol designation of China.

The seven steps

1Decide what to protect — Latin and Chinese-character marks

Most foreign brands should consider two filings: the original Latin/English mark and a Chinese-character version. Chinese consumers and platforms search and refer to brands by their Chinese names; if you do not register a translation or transliteration, a third party may do so and block your store or listings.

Word marks give the broadest protection. Stylized logos can be filed separately, but a logo registration does not protect the words in other fonts.

2Run a clearance search (don't skip this)

Search CNIPA records for identical and similar marks in the relevant classes. Chinese examiners compare not only exact matches but also similar pronunciation, appearance and meaning, including Chinese-character equivalents. A search before filing reduces the chance of a costly refusal and surfaces squatters early.

You can run a first-pass self-check with our free China trademark tools, then have an agent review the borderline results.

3Choose your classes using Chinese standard items

China uses the Nice Classification, but CNIPA applies its own standard items (Chinese goods/services descriptions). You must select accepted items; a class heading alone is not enough, and coverage that works in a US/EU application may not map cleanly. Cover your current products and the closely related services sellers often overlook (e.g. retail services, online marketplace services, app software).

Our Nice class picker helps map products to the right classes and Chinese items.

4Prepare the documents

  • Applicant details: exact legal name and address (for companies, as shown on the company register).
  • A clear representation of the mark and the selected goods/services items.
  • A power of attorney (POA) to the Chinese agent — in most routine filings this is straightforward; notarization or legalization is usually not required for ordinary applications but may be needed for specific procedures.
  • For priority claims, details of the earlier foreign application (within the six-month Paris Convention window).

5File with CNIPA and pay the official fee

The agent files the application electronically. CNIPA issues an application number and begins formal examination. The standard online official fee is CNY 270 for one class (up to 10 items); additional items within a class and additional classes carry extra official charges.

Estimate the total, including agent service fees, with the CNIPA fee calculator.

6Substantive examination and publication

CNIPA examines distinctiveness, compliance and conflicts with prior rights. If there is an objection, it issues an office action (a notice of refusal or examination opinion) that must be answered within the deadline. If the application passes, it is published for a three-month opposition period, during which third parties may file an opposition.

7Registration and maintenance

With no successful opposition (and after CNIPA completes its registration formalities), a trademark registration certificate issues. Protection lasts ten years and is renewable indefinitely in ten-year periods. Watch the non-use cancellation rule: a mark can be vulnerable to cancellation if it has not been used in China for three consecutive years without a justified reason — keep evidence of use (invoices, listings, packaging, advertising).

National filing vs the Madrid Protocol

If you already hold a home application or registration, you can extend protection to China through an international registration under the Madrid System instead of filing directly. Madrid is convenient for covering several countries at once, but the China phase still applies Chinese substantive standards, and any provisional refusal must be answered in Chinese by a local agent.

  • Direct national filing often suits brands for whom China is a key market — it is more controllable, handles Chinese-character marks and tailored class items well, and avoids "central attack" risk.
  • Madrid suits applicants who already have a solid home basis and want multi-country coverage with simpler administration.

What if your brand is already registered by someone else?

You usually have options depending on the timing and facts:

  • Oppose during the three-month publication window.
  • File for invalidation of a bad-faith registration.
  • Non-use cancellation where the mark has sat unused for three years.
  • Negotiate an assignment (buy the mark) as a pragmatic route.
  • Claim stronger protection in clear cases, supported by evidence of reputation and the squatter's bad faith.

The sooner a problem is spotted, the cheaper the fix. Monitoring new filings and publications is the most cost-effective step.

Frequently asked questions

Can a foreign company or individual own a Chinese trademark?

Yes. A foreign entity can own a Chinese registration, but without a local residence or business address the application must be submitted through a CNIPA-recorded Chinese agent.

How long does it take to register a trademark in China?

A smooth application typically takes about 6–9 months from filing to registration. Office actions, a refusal review or an opposition can extend this considerably.

How much does it cost?

The standard official online fee is CNY 270 for the first class (up to 10 items), with additional charges for extra items and classes, plus the agent's service fee. Use the fee calculator for an estimate.

Do I really need the Chinese-character version?

It is strongly recommended. Chinese shoppers and platforms use Chinese names; an unregistered transliteration is often taken by a third party and can then be used to block your listings.

Is "first to file" absolute — is there any protection for prior use?

China is first-to-file, but the law includes limited safeguards against bad-faith filings and squatting, and certain prior-use situations may be argued. These are exceptions and require evidence; relying on them is far riskier than filing early.

After registration, do I have to use the mark?

Yes. A registration can be cancelled for three consecutive years of non-use without a justified reason. Keep dated evidence of genuine use in China.
